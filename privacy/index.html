Privacy Policy

The short version

  • Margin keeps what it needs to list and manage your eBay shop: your account, your eBay connection, your listings and your orders.

1. Who we are

Margin is a Chrome extension, a website with a dashboard, and a server that connects them to eBay. It is run by a sole trader in England and Wales, United Kingdom, trading as Margin (“Margin”, “we”, “us”).

This policy covers the extension, this website and its dashboard, and Margin’s server. For the personal data it describes, we are the controller under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018. The one exception is your eBay buyers’ data, which we handle for you (section 3).

Questions about this policy or your data: s77506910@gmail.com.

2. What we collect and why

Your account

When you create an account: your email address, the username you choose, and your password. The password itself is never stored. The server keeps only a salted hash of it. We also keep the names you give your shops, your plan, your appearance choices (so the dashboard can match the panel), your Overview layouts, when you last signed in and were last active, and your sessions, stored as hashes of the session token. We use these to run your account and keep it secure.

If we act on your account from Margin’s admin page, for example to suspend it or to set its plan, we keep what was done, when, by whom and why.

Your eBay connection

When you connect an eBay account, eBay gives Margin tokens so it can act for you. They are stored on the server, encrypted, and are never sent to the extension or written to logs. We also record your eBay user ID and username and the permissions eBay granted, which is what lets us act on eBay’s account-deletion notices.

Your shop’s data from eBay

To show and manage your shop, the server reads from eBay for you: your listings (item numbers, SKUs, titles, pictures, prices and quantities), your orders, feedback, returns and traffic, your account type and selling limit, and whether Out of Stock Control is on. Your orders include your buyers’ names, postal addresses and phone numbers as eBay supplies them, because you need them to ship. The server keeps the latest answer to each of these for a short time (section 7). When you press Accept return, Refund or Send refund and confirm it, the server passes that to eBay; nothing is sent to eBay about a return or a refund without that press.

Your listings, prices and stock

The extension keeps a record of each product you list: the product code and store, the product page’s address, the eBay SKU and listing IDs, the title, the last price and stock seen, and when it was last checked. It also keeps a log of changes (price moves, stock changes, and every change Margin made to an eBay listing, with eBay’s reply) and any item costs you type in.

Every 30 minutes the extension shares these records, your costs and the latest 500 log entries with the server, so the dashboard shows what the panel shows and your other computers stay in step. We also keep which orders you marked as bought, with the order number and tracking the extension read. The extension makes a random install ID on first run, which is used to move what you listed before signing in into your shop.

The heartbeat

After each round of checks, and every 30 minutes, the extension tells the server the time, how many products it read, your price rule with its fee settings, your check settings (whether Margin follows stock and price, your minimum profit, the quantity to restore, and your low-stock and delivery limits), and, if you turned on the safety net, its number of hours. This lets the dashboard work out profit the same way the panel does, tell you when checks have stopped running, and lets the checks below act exactly as your browser would while it is closed.

Checks while your computer is off

When your browser hasn’t been heard from for an hour, Margin’s server checks your AliExpress listings itself, through AliExpress’s own API and the AliExpress connection you already made. It asks AliExpress about those products by item number, and acts on the answer with your own settings, as your browser would: a sold-out product goes to 0 on eBay, and on a plan with automatic rules a listing is repriced or put back on sale. Nothing new is sent anywhere. Each listing it checks is marked “Checked by Margin’s server”.

The safety net, only if you turn it on in the panel: when your browser hasn’t been heard from for the number of hours you chose, the server sets your checked listings to 0 on eBay, through eBay’s out-of-stock control, so nothing sells that may have sold out. It never ends a listing, and it leaves alone the listings checked in that time by the server or by other sellers’ browsers.

The extension can also keep checking after you close Chrome’s windows, while Chrome runs in the background. That changes nothing about what it reads or sends.

Helping other sellers, only if you turn it on

Help other sellers (shared checking) is off until you turn it on, and you can turn it off at any time. It works both ways: only shops that help are helped.

  • When you help, while your browser is open, the extension asks our server for a few Amazon products to check (at most 20 an hour and 100 a day). It reads each product page from your internet connection without your Amazon cookies, so Amazon sees a signed-out visit, not your account. It sends our server only the price, whether the product can be bought, and whether the page is gone. Amazon sees these page requests coming from your internet connection, as it would if you opened the pages yourself.
  • What a helper is told is only the Amazon site and the product’s code and page. Never who the seller is, their shop, price, margin or the listing’s title. Some of the products are ones we already know the answer to, so we can tell when a helper’s answers are wrong.
  • When you are helped, after your browser has been quiet for two hours, the Amazon product codes of your listings (never your name, shop, prices or margins) are given to other helpers’ browsers. Margin acts only when two different helpers agree: a product that sold out goes to 0, and a price that went up can be repriced upward on a plan with automatic rules. A price drop or a product back in stock is only shown to you. Listings checked this way are marked “Checked by other sellers’ browsers”.
  • What we keep about each check: the product, the answer, which account and shop sent it, and a one-way, salted code made from the sender’s IP address, used only to tell whether two answers came from the same connection. We never store the IP address itself. We delete these records after 7 days. A helper whose answers keep disagreeing with other helpers is stopped from helping, and we keep a note of that and why.

Product pages the extension reads

When you open a product page on a store Margin supports, the extension reads it in your own browser, signed in as you, just as if you had opened the page yourself. The page is not sent to our server to be read. What you choose to list (title, description, item specifics, pictures and price) goes to the server and on to eBay as your listing. The extension looks at the address of the tab you are on only to know whether it is a product page. It doesn’t keep a history of the pages you visit.

Pictures for your listings

To publish a listing, Margin copies its pictures to cloud storage that eBay fetches them from. eBay copies them onto its own servers when you publish, and our copies are deleted after 30 days.

AliExpress, if you connect it

When you connect AliExpress, its own sign-in page asks you to approve Margin. We never see your AliExpress password. AliExpress’s tokens are stored on the server, encrypted, with your AliExpress account’s display name. The server then asks AliExpress’s API about the products you check or list, by item number, and, for the Find tab, for the best sellers of the departments you pick and the words you search for, and passes the answers to your browser without keeping them.

Ordering on AliExpress, if you use it

If you press Order on AliExpress on an order, or turn on Order on AliExpress automatically, the server places an order on your AliExpress account for that eBay sale, shipped to your buyer. To do that, it sends AliExpress your buyer’s name, address and phone number, with the product, option and quantity sold. Margin doesn’t store the buyer’s address: it goes from eBay’s answer straight into the order.

We keep a record of each order (the eBay order and line numbers, the AliExpress product and order numbers, the price, status, tracking and dates) so that no order is placed twice and its tracking can be sent to eBay. With Pay automatically on, AliExpress charges the payment method saved in your AliExpress account. Margin never sees it.

Buying at other stores

When you buy an order on Amazon from the Orders tab, the extension fills your buyer’s address into the checkout in your own browser. It keeps that one address in your browser until the order is done, for two hours at most. You place and pay for the order yourself.

The leaderboard, if you join it

If you put your account on the leaderboard, other signed-in Margin sellers see the name you give it there and its figures from eBay, added up across all the shops on your account: items sold, orders, listings for sale and which eBay site. Your sales total is shown only if you choose, and so, separately, is your profit: your sales minus eBay’s fees and the cost of the items sold, worked out the way your dashboard works it out, from the item costs Margin already has for you. Where some costs or fees aren’t known, they’re left out, and other sellers see how many were known. The cost of any one item is never shown. Leaving, from any of your shops, deletes all of it right away.

Error reports, only if you turn them on

Error reports are off until you turn them on, separately in the panel and on the website. When they are on and something in Margin breaks, it sends the error, where in Margin it happened, the screen, the Margin version, your browser’s name and version, the time, and a random ID used for nothing else. Email addresses, tokens, long numbers, postcodes, IP addresses and web addresses are removed before a report is sent, and again when it arrives. Reports are not linked to your account.

Password-reset emails

If you ask for a password-reset link, your email address is sent to Resend, which delivers that one email. Until email sending is switched on, Margin’s operator can make a link and send it to you by hand. A link works once, for one hour.

Security limits

To stop password guessing and abuse, the server counts sign-ins, sign-ups, password resets and error reports by IP address and by email address.

Payments

If you choose a paid plan, Stripe is our payment processor. You type your card details on Stripe’s own checkout page, and Stripe holds them: they never reach Margin. From Stripe we keep your Stripe customer ID, which plan you pay for, when your paid period ends, and whether your subscription is set to end. Stripe tells us when a payment goes through, fails, or a subscription changes or ends, and handles your payment under its own privacy policy. If you delete your Margin account, any subscription is canceled at Stripe first.

3. Your eBay buyers’ data

Your buyers’ names, addresses and phone numbers reach Margin because you connected your eBay shop and use Margin to handle your orders. For that data, you are the controller and Margin is your processor. We use it only to provide Margin to you: to show your orders, fill in a checkout address in your browser, place the AliExpress orders you ask for, and send tracking to eBay. It goes nowhere except the marketplaces it comes from and goes to.

You are responsible for your own duties to your buyers under data protection law, such as telling them how their data is used. Our Terms of Use set out how we handle it for you.

4. Our lawful bases

  • Contract. To provide Margin under our Terms of Use: your account, your eBay and AliExpress connections, listings, checks, orders, pictures and the dashboard.
  • Legitimate interests. To keep Margin secure and working, such as rate limits, the admin log and recording failures in the server’s own requests, and to handle support requests and disputes. We use as little data as we can for these.
  • Consent. For error reports, the leaderboard and helping other sellers. You can withdraw it at any time by turning error reports off, leaving the leaderboard or turning Help other sellers off.
  • Legal obligation. Where the law requires us to keep or disclose information.

5. Who receives your data

These service providers run parts of Margin for us and process data on our instructions:

  • Cloudflare runs Margin’s server (Cloudflare Workers) and stores listing pictures (Cloudflare R2).
  • Turso hosts Margin’s database, in the United States.
  • Vercel hosts this website.
  • Resend sends password-reset emails, once email sending is switched on.
  • Stripe processes payments for paid plans, only if you choose one.

These services receive data because you connect them or use them, and their own privacy policies apply:

  • eBay, which Margin reads from and writes to through eBay’s APIs, at your instruction.
  • AliExpress, through the AliExpress Open Platform, only if you connect it.
  • The stores whose pages you open, which see ordinary requests from your own browser.
  • Other Margin sellers, only if you put your account on the leaderboard.
  • Other Margin sellers’ browsers, only if you turn on Help other sellers: the Amazon product codes and pages of your listings, never who you are.
  • Amazon, when you help: it sees signed-out page requests from your internet connection for the products your browser checks.

We may also disclose data where the law requires it. We never sell or rent personal data.

6. International transfers

Margin is run from the UK, but its providers process data outside it. The database is in the United States. Cloudflare runs the server in data centers around the world, usually the one nearest the request. Vercel, Resend and Stripe are US companies, and eBay and AliExpress process data in their own countries.

When personal data leaves the UK, we rely on the protections UK data protection law allows, such as adequacy regulations (including the UK–US data bridge, where a provider is certified under it) or the International Data Transfer Addendum to the standard contractual clauses in a provider’s data processing terms.

7. How long we keep it

  • Your account: until you delete it.
  • Sessions: 30 days after you last used them, or until you sign out.
  • Password-reset links: usable for one hour, and removed the next time a link is made for your account or when you delete it.
  • A username you give up: held for 7 days, so nobody else can take it right away.
  • eBay and AliExpress connections: until you disconnect, remove the shop or delete your account. An eBay account-deletion notice removes the eBay connection too.
  • What eBay said about your shop: the latest answer is kept for reuse, for 5 minutes for orders (with your buyers’ details), an hour for the returns card and 15 minutes for each return (with the buyer’s eBay username and what they wrote), 3 hours for the list of your listings, 6 hours for feedback, until the end of the day (UTC) for traffic, and a day for your account type and selling limit. It stays until the next read replaces it, or until you disconnect eBay, remove the shop or delete your account.
  • Listing records, the change log, item costs and order marks: until you delete your account. Removing a shop keeps its records, so a mis-click can’t erase its history. Notes of removed listings and order marks are dropped after 120 days.
  • AliExpress order records: until you disconnect AliExpress, remove the shop or delete your account.
  • The heartbeat: only the latest one.
  • Helping other sellers: each check’s record 7 days. Whether you help, how your answers compared with other helpers’, and any stop from helping: until you remove the shop or delete your account.
  • Listing pictures: 30 days.
  • The leaderboard: until you leave it.
  • Error reports: 30 days after the error last happened, and 500 at most.
  • Security counters: each is replaced the next time the same IP address or email is counted. The ones that carry your email are deleted with your account.
  • The admin log: the newest 5,000 entries. When an account is deleted, its entries stay with the email cut to its domain and names removed.
  • In your browser: the extension keeps its data until you uninstall it, and signing out removes that shop’s orders. The website keeps its data until you sign out or clear your browser’s storage.

Deleting your account removes your account and, for every shop on it, its eBay and AliExpress connections, listing records, change log, costs, order marks, pictures, its part in helping other sellers and everything kept about its eBay account, straight away. Your listings on eBay are not touched: they stay live, and Margin simply stops managing them.

8. What Margin doesn’t do

  • No analytics, tracking pixels, advertising IDs or third-party scripts, in the extension or on this website. Even the fonts are served from this site.
  • No selling or renting of your data, and no use of it for advertising.
  • No use of your data to decide your creditworthiness or for lending.

9. Cookies and browser storage

Margin sets no cookies. The landing page stores nothing in your browser. The dashboard keeps a few things in your browser’s local storage: your session, the shop you picked, your Overview layouts, your theme and chart choices, whether error reports are on and which were sent, and a note if you signed out there.

The extension keeps its data in Chrome’s storage on your computer, not synced to your Google account. Uninstalling it deletes that data. None of this is used to track you.

10. Security

  • Passwords are stored only as salted hashes (PBKDF2-SHA256, 210,000 rounds), and session tokens as SHA-256 hashes.
  • eBay and AliExpress tokens are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, and never leave the server.
  • Data travels between your browser, this website and the server over HTTPS.
  • Every request is checked against the signed-in account, so one seller can’t reach another’s shop.
  • Sign-in and reset attempts are limited, and only Margin’s operator can reach the admin page.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach puts your data at risk, we will tell you, and the Information Commissioner’s Office where the law requires.

11. Your rights

Under UK data protection law, you have the right to:

  • Access your personal data and get a copy of it.
  • Correct it. You can change your username and password on the dashboard’s Account page. For anything else, such as your email address, contact us.
  • Erase it. Delete account on the dashboard’s Account page deletes your account and what Margin keeps for it straight away.
  • Restrict how we use it.
  • Take it with you. The extension’s Backup, in Settings, exports your settings, listings and costs as a file, and you can ask us for the rest.
  • Object to our use of it based on legitimate interests.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, where we rely on it.
  • Complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or on 0303 123 1113. We would appreciate the chance to put things right first.

To use any of these rights, email s77506910@gmail.com from the address on your account. It’s free, we may need to confirm it’s you, and we will reply within one month.

12. Children

Margin is for businesses and is not for anyone under 18. We don’t knowingly collect data about children. If you think a child has made an account, contact us and we will delete it.

13. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as Margin changes. The date at the top says when it last changed. If a change significantly affects how we use your data, we will tell you by email or in the dashboard before it takes effect.
