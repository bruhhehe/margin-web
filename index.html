How Margin works

  1. 01

    Find a product

    Open a product page at any store Margin reads, and press Alt+E.

  2. 02

    Import it

    Margin imports the title, images, price, variations and specifications. Anything missing is listed by name.

  3. 03

    Set your profit

    Choose a margin or a fixed amount. Margin calculates the listing price from it and adds eBay’s fee.

  4. 04

    Publish to eBay

    Review the listing, fix anything flagged, and publish. Nothing goes to eBay until you click Publish.

  5. 05

    Margin keeps watch

    When the store changes its price or stock, Margin can update your listing to match, or set it to out of stock until the store restocks.

It scans the page you have open.

It runs in your browser, signed in as you. There is no scraper for a store to block and no server to send your account to. It reads the same fields from 22 Amazon sites and AliExpress.

Hundreds at a time, not one

Paste links, product codes or a spreadsheet column. Or open a search, a best-seller list, a category or a storefront and import every product on it, page after page.

Duplicates caught before they cost you

Anything you have listed before is flagged and skipped. You trim the batch yourself before Margin prepares any listings.

Every field it reads from a page
Title
Trimmed to eBay’s 80-character limit at a word break, with the store’s padding removed
Brand
Taken from the byline, in eleven forms across five languages, and from the details table if the byline has none
Price
The buy-box price. Struck-through list prices are ignored, and the decimal separator is read, not assumed
In stock
Read from the availability line, never from the price. If the wording is unfamiliar, Margin treats it as out of stock
Photos
One per gallery position, at full size. Sprites and placeholder pixels are dropped
Specifications
The tables, the bullet points and the breadcrumb — which is also what picks the eBay category
Variations
The full color × size grid, each one with its own product code, photos and stock
Anything it could not find
Listed by name, with the part of the page it looked in, so you can fill it in yourself.

Four things the extension adds that this calculator leaves out.

  • A price floor. It will not list below it, no matter what the math says.
  • Rounding that only goes up. So a rounded price never cuts into your profit.
  • A profit floor. A reprice that would lose money is blocked, not published.
  • Your own percentage. For Promoted Listings and anything else eBay takes off the same sale.

You can also replace any rate with the percentage and fixed fee from your own eBay invoice, which is more accurate than any published table.

It keeps watching after you list.

The source price moves, the source runs out, your listing sells out. Three switches, all off until you turn them on, checking as often as every 15 minutes.

  1. The store changes the price

    Margin calculates a new eBay price from the new cost, using the same profit rule and that listing’s own category. If the new price would leave you less than your profit floor, Margin does not send it. It flags the listing and keeps the old price.

  2. The store runs out of stock

    Margin sets your eBay quantity to zero, and restores it when the store has stock again.

  3. Your eBay listing sells out

    If eBay is holding a listing at zero because it sold out, and the store still has stock, Margin restores it to the quantity you first published.

Notifications name the product, not its product code. Forty price changes arrive as one notification, not forty.

Orders and shipping

Every sale shows what to buy, where to ship it, and when it is due. You buy the stock and you ship it. Margin handles the listing, the price and the monitoring.

  1. See what is due

    Closest deadline first, overdue in red. The count shows on the toolbar icon, so you see new sales without opening the panel.

  2. Buy it without retyping

    Opens the product page with the quantity set and fills in the buyer’s address at checkout. You click Buy now.

  3. Send tracking to eBay

    Margin reads the tracking number from your order and detects the carrier. Send one order, or all of them at once.

Your seller data stays yours.

Margin holds a key to your eBay shop. Here is what it does with it.

  • eBay tokens encrypted at rest, on the server, never in the extension
  • No analytics and no third-party calls, on this page or in the panel
  • No Amazon API. Product pages are read in your own browser, signed in as you
  • Nothing leaves your browser except to Margin and to eBay
  • Your data is never sold. There is no other business model here

Questions people ask first.

Is this allowed under eBay’s dropshipping rules?

eBay allows dropshipping if you fulfill orders from a wholesale supplier, but not if another retailer ships the item directly to your buyer. Margin is built for the first kind of seller: you hold the stock and you ship it. Read eBay’s own policy before you list.

Is there anything to set up?

No. Install it, create an account, connect your eBay shop, and list. Margin runs the server. You do not deploy or configure anything.

Your eBay refresh token is encrypted and stored on that server, not in the extension, because a browser extension is the wrong place to keep the key to your shop.

How accurate is the fee?

It is an estimate, and it has to be. eBay calculates the real fee only after the sale happens, and tells you afterward. You have to price the listing before that, so this is the best figure to price against.

Rates come from each site’s published table, per category, in all four formats eBay uses, with the regulatory operating fee included. eBay UK’s rates are checked against real order statements. That is why you can add the extra percentage your invoice shows, for Promoted Listings and anything else eBay charges without publishing a rate. After a sale, the Orders tab shows what eBay actually charged.

Which eBay sites does it work on?

Fourteen: the United Kingdom, United States, Ireland, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, Poland, Canada and Australia. Each has its own currency, fee table and site ID.

Business and private sellers pay different fees, so Margin asks eBay and remembers the answer for a day. A private seller on eBay UK pays no final value fee. On eBay US, they pay what a business pays. If eBay does not say, Margin assumes business. Quoting no fee when you will be charged one is the expensive way to be wrong.

Does it use Amazon’s API?

No — neither the Product Advertising API nor the Creators API. Margin reads pages in your own browser, signed in as you. That is also the only approach that works: Amazon shows a captcha instead of a product page to requests from a data center.
