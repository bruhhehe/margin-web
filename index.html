Listing is the quick part. Keeping it right is the work.

Three things cost an eBay seller time and money. Margin handles each of them.

  1. Typing every listing by hand

    Title, photos, item specifics, variations, and a price that covers eBay’s fee: about fifteen minutes a product.

    Margin drafts it from the product page in one press, priced from your margin with eBay’s fee for your site and category in it.

  2. Prices that move

    The store raises its price, your eBay price stays where it was, and the next sale loses money.

    Margin checks the product page and shows each listing’s profit at today’s price. On Pro and Max it reprices from your rule, never below the minimum profit you set.

  3. Stock that runs out

    The store sells out, your listing stays up, a buyer orders, and you cancel. Canceled sales count against your seller performance on eBay.

    When the product sells out, Margin sets your listing to 0 on every plan. It stays on eBay, out of stock, with its watchers and sales history.

    Fix anything flagged and publish. From then on, Margin checks the product page and keeps your price and stock in step with it.

From the product page to the parcel

One extension beside the page you are on, and one dashboard for the whole shop.

  • Drafted from the page

    The title trimmed to eBay’s 80 characters, photos at full size, item specifics suggested with where each came from, and every variation in one listing. A missing price is asked for, never guessed.

  • eBay’s fee in the price

    Each eBay site’s published fee table, per category, for business and private sellers. Set your margin as a percentage or an amount, and Show the math lays out the sum.

Careful with the shop you already have

Margin holds a key to your eBay shop. Here is what it does with it, and what it never does.

Your listings

  • Never ended by Margin. No check, plan or setting ends or withdraws a listing. Ending one is your decision, and Margin asks you to confirm it.
  • Out of stock, still listed. A 0 is sent only once eBay confirms it will keep the listing up. Until then Margin holds it and tells you.
  • A big price drop is read twice. A fall of more than 20% is checked again before anything is repriced.
  • Test mode first. A new install publishes nothing to eBay until you choose Live eBay.

Your data

  • Your eBay sign-in is encrypted on Margin’s server, and never kept in the extension.
  • Pages are read in your browser, signed in as you. No Amazon API and no scraping server.
  • No analytics, no trackers, no ads. Your data is never sold.
  • Each shop kept apart. Its listings, orders and settings stay with that shop.

Questions sellers ask first

Can Margin end or delete my eBay listings?

No. Margin never ends or withdraws a listing on its own, on any plan, for any reason. Ending one is always your decision, and Margin asks you to confirm it first.

When a product sells out, the listing goes to 0 through eBay’s out-of-stock setting and stays up. If eBay can’t confirm that setting is on, Margin holds the 0 and tells you, because eBay ends a listing that reaches 0 without it.

What happens when a product sells out or its price changes?

Margin checks each product page on a schedule: once a day on Free, every hour on Pro, and every 15 minutes on Max. A sold-out product goes to 0 on every plan. On Pro and Max it goes back on sale by itself once two checks in a row agree the product is back.

On Pro and Max, a price change reprices the listing from your rule, never below your minimum profit. A drop of more than 20% is read twice before anything changes. The checks run in your browser, so none run while Chrome is closed.

Can I try it without anything going live?

Yes. A new install starts in test mode: listings are checked and saved exactly as they would be published, and nothing reaches eBay. The panel always shows which mode it’s in. When you’re ready, set Publish to Live eBay in Settings.

Is my data safe?

Your eBay sign-in is stored encrypted on Margin’s server, never in the extension. Product pages are read in your own browser, signed in as you. There are no analytics, trackers or ads, error reports stay off unless you turn them on, and your data is never sold.

You can delete your account, and everything Margin keeps for it, from the dashboard. The Privacy Policy has the details.

Does it work with my eBay account?

It works on fourteen eBay sites: the United Kingdom, United States, Ireland, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, Poland, Canada and Australia, each in its own currency with its own fee table.

Business and private sellers pay different fees, so Margin asks eBay which you are. A private seller on eBay UK pays no final value fee; on eBay US, they pay what a business pays. If eBay doesn’t say, Margin prices as a business, because quoting no fee when you will be charged one is the expensive way to be wrong. One account holds one eBay shop on Free and Pro, and up to five on Max.

Which stores can I list from?

Amazon, on 22 of its sites, and AliExpress. Walmart, Temu, Alibaba and OneBuy are marked soon on this page and don’t work yet.

Margin doesn’t use Amazon’s API. It reads pages in your own browser, signed in as you, which is also the only way that works: Amazon shows a captcha instead of a product page to requests from a data center.

How accurate is the fee?

It is an estimate, and it has to be. eBay works out the real fee after the sale and tells you afterward, and you have to price the listing before that, so this is the best figure to price against.

Rates come from each site’s published table, per category, with the regulatory operating fee included. eBay UK’s rates are checked against real order statements. If your invoice says otherwise, you can price against the percentage and fixed fee it shows instead. After a sale, the Orders tab shows what eBay actually charged.

What happens if I cancel?

Cancel any time from your dashboard. Your listings stay on eBay whatever plan you’re on: a plan never ends a listing. Moving to a smaller plan only limits what comes next, such as a new listing past the plan’s limit. Free has no expiration.

Is this allowed under eBay’s rules?

eBay has its own rules on where an item may be bought and who ships it to your buyer, and they can differ by site. eBay restricts buying an item from another retailer or marketplace and having it shipped straight to your buyer. The Margin panel reminds you on every screen: list only stock you hold and ship yourself.

Margin doesn’t check your listings or orders against eBay’s rules, and following them is up to you. Read eBay’s policy for each site you sell on before you list, and see section 7 of the Terms of Use.
