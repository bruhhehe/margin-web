It reads the page you already have open.

Margin reads the product page inside your own browser, signed in as you, in a hidden tab that never steals your focus. It does not use Amazon’s API and it does not send anything to a server to be scraped — Amazon serves a captcha to datacentre traffic anyway. It works on 22 Amazon sites and on AliExpress, and gets the same fields out of all of them.

List up to 40 products at once

Paste links or product codes into the Bulk tab, or let it take every product off the Amazon search, best-seller list or storefront you have open. It reads three at a time and shows you which step each one is on. Products you have already listed are flagged and skipped. You review the batch before anything goes live, and closing the panel does not lose it.

It does not hammer Amazon

Three requests at a time, 250 ms apart, no matter what else is running. If a price comes back more than 20% away from the last one, Margin re-reads the page slowly and in full before it believes it.

The price already covers eBay’s fee.

Type the profit you want and Margin works backwards from it. It looks up eBay’s rate for your site and category, then finds the price that pays the fee and still leaves your profit. Then it checks the rate again at that price, because some categories charge more above a threshold and the first answer can push the sale over it.

target = what you pay + the profit you want + your own costs price = (target + eBay’s fixed fee) ÷ (1 − eBay’s rate)

eBay charges a different rate by category, so this answer is only as good as the category you picked above. In the extension there is no guessing: it uses the category you are actually listing in.

The extension does four more things this calculator does not. It will not list below a price you set. It rounds prices up, never down, so rounding never eats the profit. It refuses a repricing that would drop you below a profit floor instead of publishing it at a loss. And you can add a percentage for Promoted Listings and anything else eBay takes off the same sale but does not publish. You can also replace any rate with the percentage and fixed fee from your own eBay invoice, which beats any published table.

Margin keeps checking the Amazon page after you list.

Every 15 minutes, or every few hours — you choose. You also set how small a price move it should ignore. What it does about a change is three separate switches, and all three start switched off: a listing that changes its own price is one you have to trust first.

  1. Amazon changes the price

    Margin works out a new eBay price from the new cost, using the same profit rule and that listing’s own category. If the new price would leave you less than your profit floor, it does not send it — it flags the listing and leaves the old price alone.

  2. Amazon runs out of stock

    Margin sets your eBay quantity to zero, and puts it back when Amazon has stock again. A price that has simply disappeared from the page does not count as a price of zero, and nothing acts on it.

  3. Your eBay listing sells out

    If eBay is holding a listing at zero because it sold, and Amazon still has stock, Margin puts it back up at the quantity you first published. It reads what eBay actually holds before it does anything, so it only touches listings that really are at zero.

  4. Amazon starts blocking the checks

    Five listings in a row that worked last time and fail now means Amazon is blocking you, not that five products have gone bad. Margin stops the run there, leaves the rest of your listings untouched, tells you on screen and in a notification, and tries again in thirty minutes.

It only touches what changed: a price move never resets your quantity, and a stock change never re-prices the listing. Notifications name the product, not its product code, and forty price changes arrive as one notification rather than forty.

Four things Margin will not do.

  • It will not buy the item for you.

    From an order, “Order on Amazon” opens the product page with the quantity already set and types the buyer’s address into the checkout form when it appears. It never presses Buy now — that click stays yours — and it never dispatches anything.

  • It will not guess — at a fee or at anything else.

    If a price is missing, Margin asks you for it rather than making one up. It suggests item specifics and shows you where each one came from, and only writes them once you accept. If it cannot work out the right eBay category, it hands you eBay’s own suggestions instead of picking the nearest thing.

  • It will not touch a listing you did not ask it to.

    Nothing goes live until you approve a confirmation box showing the title, price, quantity, condition, category, delivery, number of photos and SKU. A new install starts in test mode, where a listing is checked and recorded but never reaches eBay, and the panel always shows which mode you are in — test, sandbox or live.

  • It will not send your work anywhere.

    No analytics, no third-party calls. Nothing leaves your browser except to the server you deployed and to eBay itself. Your eBay tokens are encrypted and kept on that server, never in the extension.

What it costs.

Three plans. The difference is how much Margin does without asking you first. On Free you list by hand and Margin tells you what has changed. On Pro it acts on the change itself — new price, off sale, back on sale — and lists a whole page of search results in one pass. Max is for running more than one eBay shop from one login.

Questions people ask first.

Can I install it today?

Not from the Chrome Web Store — the listing is written but not published yet. The extension itself is built and works today; you load it from a folder instead, and the three steps are at the foot of this page. The dashboard is live right now, free, and needs nothing installed. This page will carry the store link the day there is one.

Is this allowed under eBay’s dropshipping rules?

eBay allows dropshipping if you fulfil orders from a wholesale supplier. It does not allow you to list an item and have another retailer or marketplace post it straight to your buyer, and it can end your listings or restrict your account if you do. That is eBay’s rule, not ours — read it in their words before you list anything.

Margin is built for sellers who hold their own stock and post it themselves. It never places an order, never presses Buy now and never posts anything. Before you publish, the confirmation box states that you hold this stock and will dispatch it yourself. Margin does nothing to hide what a listing is from eBay. How you actually run your business is down to you.

Do I have to run a server?

To publish to eBay, yes. Your eBay credentials and refresh token have to live somewhere safer than a browser extension, so you deploy a small server and point Margin at its address. It runs on Cloudflare Workers’ free plan, or on Node on your own machine.

To try it, no. A new install starts in test mode, which needs no server at all: it reads pages, builds drafts and prices them on your machine, and never calls eBay.

How accurate is the fee?

It is an estimate, and it has to be: eBay only works out the real fee once the sale exists, and tells you afterwards. But you have to price the listing before that, so this is the best figure to price against.

The rates come from each site’s own published fee table, per category, in all four shapes eBay uses: a flat rate, a rate that changes above a threshold, a whole sale re-rated at one rate, and banded tiers. Where a site charges a regulatory operating fee, that is included too. We checked eBay UK’s table against real order statements from a working seller account — which is why there is a setting for the extra percentage an invoice shows on top, for Promoted Listings and anything else eBay takes off the same sale without publishing a rate. After a sale, the Orders tab shows what eBay actually charged.

Which eBay sites does it work on?

Fourteen: the United Kingdom, United States, Ireland, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, Poland, Canada and Australia. Each has its own currency, fee table and site id.

Whether you are a business or a private seller changes the fee enough that Margin asks eBay and remembers the answer for a day. A private seller on eBay UK pays no final value fee at all; a private seller on eBay US pays the same as a business. If eBay will not say, Margin assumes business — quoting you no fee when you will be charged one is the expensive way to be wrong.

Does it use Amazon’s API?

No — neither the Product Advertising API nor the Creators API. Margin reads pages in your own browser, signed in as you. That is also the only approach that works: Amazon serves a captcha to a datacentre address instead of a product page.

What do the plans cost?

Free covers 25 live listings on one eBay shop: you list by hand, and Margin tells you what has changed. Pro, at £19 a month, is where Margin starts acting on what it finds — re-pricing when Amazon’s price moves, taking a listing off sale when the stock goes, and listing a page of search results in one pass. Max, at £39, adds up to twelve eBay shops on one login.

Your plan pays for Margin itself. The server you run it on is separate and also yours: a Cloudflare Workers account and a database, both of which have free tiers this fits inside. There is no contract, and you cancel from the dashboard.

How to run it today.

It is not on the Chrome Web Store yet, so you load it from a folder instead of installing it from a store. Three steps, and it opens beside whatever page you are on.

  1. Build it. Clone the repository and run npm install, then npm run build -w @margin/extension. That writes a dist folder.
  2. Load it. Open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, press Load unpacked, and choose that dist folder.
  3. Open it. Alt+E, or the toolbar icon, on whichever tab you were last on.
