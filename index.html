It reads the page you already have open.

Margin reads the product page inside your own browser, signed in as you, in a hidden tab that never steals your focus. It does not use Amazon’s API and it does not send anything to a server to be scraped — Amazon serves a captcha to datacentre traffic anyway. It works on 22 Amazon sites and on AliExpress, and gets the same fields out of all of them.

Title
Trimmed to eBay’s 80-character limit at a word break, with Amazon’s padding removed
Brand
Taken from the byline, in eleven languages, and from the details table if the byline has none
Price
The buy-box price. Struck-through list prices are ignored, and the decimal separator is read, not assumed
In stock
Read from the availability line, never from the price. If the wording is unfamiliar, Margin calls it out of stock
Photos
One per gallery position, at full size. Sprites and placeholder pixels are dropped
Specifications
The tables, the bullet points and the breadcrumb — which is also what picks the eBay category
Variations
The full colour × size grid, each one with its own product code, photos and stock
Anything it could not find
Listed by name, with the part of the page it looked in, so you can fill it in yourself.

List up to 40 products at once

Paste links or product codes into the Bulk tab, or let it take every product off the Amazon search, best-seller list or storefront you have open. It reads three at a time and shows you which step each one is on. Products you have already listed are flagged and skipped. You review the batch before anything goes live, and closing the panel does not lose it.

A run of forty finishes

Margin reads three products at a time with a pause between them, so a bulk run gets to the end instead of stalling halfway. If a price comes back more than 20% away from the last one, it re-reads the page in full before it believes it.

The price already covers eBay’s fee.

Type the profit you want and Margin works backwards from it. It looks up eBay’s rate for your site and category, then finds the price that pays the fee and still leaves your profit. Then it checks the rate again at that price, because some categories charge more above a threshold and the first answer can push the sale over it.

target = what you pay + the profit you want + your own costs price = (target + eBay’s fixed fee) ÷ (1 − eBay’s rate)

eBay charges a different rate by category, so this answer is only as good as the category you picked above. The extension uses the category you are actually listing in.

The extension adds four things this calculator leaves out: a price floor it will not list below, rounding that only ever goes up so it never eats the profit, a profit floor that blocks a re-price instead of publishing one at a loss, and a percentage of your own for Promoted Listings and anything else eBay takes off the same sale. You can also replace any rate with the percentage and fixed fee from your own eBay invoice, which beats any published table.

Margin keeps checking the Amazon page after you list.

Every 15 minutes, or every few hours — you choose. You also set how small a price move it should ignore. Each of the three things below is its own switch, and they all start off.

  1. Amazon changes the price

    Margin works out a new eBay price from the new cost, using the same profit rule and that listing’s own category. If the new price would leave you less than your profit floor, it does not send it — it flags the listing and leaves the old price alone.

  2. Amazon runs out of stock

    Margin sets your eBay quantity to zero, and puts it back when Amazon has stock again.

  3. Your eBay listing sells out

    If eBay is holding a listing at zero because it sold, and Amazon still has stock, Margin puts it back up at the quantity you first published.

Notifications name the product rather than its product code, and forty price changes arrive as one notification rather than forty.

Two things to know before you start.

  • You buy the stock and you post it.

    Margin handles the listing, the price and the watching. When an order comes in, “Order on Amazon” opens the product page with the quantity set and fills in the buyer’s address — then you press Buy now, and you post the parcel.

  • Your shop data stays yours.

    No analytics and no third-party calls. Nothing leaves your browser except to Margin and to eBay, and your eBay tokens are encrypted at rest rather than held in the extension.

Questions people ask first.

Is this allowed under eBay’s dropshipping rules?

eBay allows dropshipping if you fulfil orders from a wholesale supplier, but not if another retailer posts the item straight to your buyer. Margin is built for the first kind of seller: you hold the stock and you post it. Worth reading eBay’s own wording before you list.

Is there anything to set up?

No. Install it, create an account, connect your eBay shop, and list. Margin runs the server side; you do not deploy anything and there is nothing to configure.

Your eBay refresh token is encrypted and held on that server rather than in the extension, because a browser extension is the wrong place to keep the key to your shop.

How accurate is the fee?

It is an estimate, and it has to be: eBay only works out the real fee once the sale exists, and tells you afterwards. But you have to price the listing before that, so this is the best figure to price against.

The rates come from each site’s own published fee table, per category, in all four shapes eBay uses: a flat rate, a rate that changes above a threshold, a whole sale re-rated at one rate, and banded tiers. Where a site charges a regulatory operating fee, that is included too. We checked eBay UK’s table against real order statements from a working seller account — which is why there is a setting for the extra percentage an invoice shows on top, for Promoted Listings and anything else eBay takes off the same sale without publishing a rate. After a sale, the Orders tab shows what eBay actually charged.

Which eBay sites does it work on?

Fourteen: the United Kingdom, United States, Ireland, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Switzerland, Poland, Canada and Australia. Each has its own currency, fee table and site id.

Whether you are a business or a private seller changes the fee enough that Margin asks eBay and remembers the answer for a day. A private seller on eBay UK pays no final value fee at all; a private seller on eBay US pays the same as a business. If eBay will not say, Margin assumes business — quoting you no fee when you will be charged one is the expensive way to be wrong.

Does it use Amazon’s API?

No — neither the Product Advertising API nor the Creators API. Margin reads pages in your own browser, signed in as you. That is also the only approach that works: Amazon serves a captcha to a datacentre address instead of a product page.

What do the plans cost?

Free covers 25 live listings on one eBay shop: you list by hand, and Margin tells you what has changed. Pro, at £19 a month, is where Margin starts acting on what it finds — re-pricing when Amazon’s price moves, taking a listing off sale when the stock goes, and listing a page of search results in one pass. Max, at £39, adds up to twelve eBay shops on one login.
